Chasing the Millisecond

The Technological Evolution of Sports Electronic Timing Systems

Late 19th - Early 20th Century

The Era of Mechanical Precision

Before automation, races relied entirely on handheld mechanical stopwatches. Human error was rampant, as reaction times between seeing a starter pistol flash and pressing a button varied dramatically, frequently resulting in disputed finishes.

1932 - 1948

The Birth of Photo-Finish

At the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics, the "Kirby camera" was introduced, simultaneously filming the finish line while recording time values. By 1948, the continuous-strip "Magic Eye" camera eradicated the human visual limitations of judging close track encounters.

1960s - 1970s

Fully Automatic Timing (FAT)

The 1968 Mexico City Olympics marked a watershed moment: electronic timing became official. Automated contact plates inside swimming pools allowed athletes to stop their own clocks, introducing unprecedented impartiality to competitive aquatics.

1990s - Present

Transponders & Quantum Accuracy

Modern sports leverage high-frequency RFID chips woven into bibs or shoes to track thousands of marathon runners simultaneously. Elite sprints now use scan-rate cameras firing up to 10,000 frames per second, measuring down to the microsecond.
